## KQ1. Metrics and benchmarks for generated-video quality: which correlate best with humans, and which run in <1 s per clip?

### Takeaway
VBench-2.0 (Mar 2025) reports model-level Spearman agreement of 82–99% per dimension with humans, but it is a *benchmark* (hours per model, VLM/LLM-backed dimensions), not a per-clip gate. FVD is widely shown to be content-biased and temporally insensitive, and should not be used as a QC signal. The only metric families with evidence of sub-second per-clip runtime are classical/CNN VQA (DOVER-Mobile ~1.4 s CPU, ~100 ms per 30-frame clip on GPU for challenge models) and embedding-similarity metrics (DINO/CLIP), while physics benchmarks (VideoPhy-2, PhyGenBench) have only moderate human agreement (ρ≈0.56 for PhyGenEval) and depend on VLMs.

### Cited Findings

**VBench / VBench-2.0 / VBench-Long**

- VBench-2.0 backbones (relevant for reuse as gate components): LLaVA-Video-7B (VQA/description alignment), Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (LLM judge), Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (clip-level anomaly / Instance Preservation), ViT-base anomaly specialists for body/hands/faces + YOLO-World detection (Human Anatomy), ArcFace + RetinaFace (identity), CoTracker-v2 (camera motion), SIFT/FLANN/RANSAC/RAFT (multi-view consistency), GPT-4o-generated expected-physics descriptions (Mechanics). - Original VBench (Nov 2023) human alignment: per-dimension Spearman ρ typically 0.82–0.998, average ≈0.94 across 16 dimensions (secondary aggregator summarizing the paper's Fig.). — [Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/fvd-and-vbench-metrics); primary: [VBench (arXiv 2311.17982)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17982), [VBench++ (arXiv 2411.13503)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13503)
- VBench Subject Consistency uses DINOv1 feature similarity across frames; it is known to be gamed by static/near-static outputs ("the first reference image dominates subsequent frames, inflating identity metrics like CLIP-I and DINO-I while eliminating meaningful motion"). — [DynSC-Eval (arXiv 2610.01052)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01052); [MV-S2V (arXiv 2601.17756)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.17756)

**World-model-specific benchmarks (drift, revisit consistency)**
- WorldScore evaluates world generation under *prescribed camera trajectories* on controllability, quality, dynamics; later papers note it "can conflate geometric inconsistency with trajectory failure when identical controls produce different observations." — [WorldRoamBench (arXiv 2606.31672)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31672v1)
- WorldRoamBench (Alibaba AMAP CV Lab, Jun 2026): 600+ test cases (Nature/Urban/Indoor, 1st & 3rd person, 10–60 s WASD interaction), 10+ models incl. Genie 3, Happy Oyster, Matrix-Game 2.0/3.0, HY-World 1.5, Yume 1.5, LingBot-World, Lyra 2.0, minWM, SANA-WM. Four axes at 0.25 weight each: (1) Action following = mean of strict per-frame accuracy, partial per-frame accuracy and TrajScore (1 − normalized translation/rotation error); (2) Visual quality = aesthetic + imaging quality with a **segment-based drift term**: split rollout into 10 equal segments, drift = (best segment mean − worst segment mean)/|best segment mean|, applied to both aesthetic and imaging scores (captures non-monotonic mid-sequence collapse missed by first-vs-last comparisons); (3) Interaction physics = mechanics (collision, clipping, deformation, terrain following, gravity) + optics (reflection, shadow) + 3D consistency, with a validity gate; (4) Memory = F1 over retained observation points vs. hallucinated revisit points (scene memory), plus Qwen3-VL-PLUS-scored subject memory for 3rd-person. "Even the best achieves only moderate scores." — [WorldRoamBench HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31672v1)
- A social-media summary says WorldRoamBench covers "1000+ long-horizon roaming samples, 12 mainstream interactive world models" and that "at 30 seconds, visual quality starts to collapse" for interactive world models — numbers differ from the paper text above; treat the tweet as secondary. — [JIQIZHIXIN on X](https://x.com/jiqizhixin/status/2102778012844408989)
- R2M-Bench (Aug 2026): 100 reference scenes × 3 leave-and-return trajectories = 300 instances; metric **MemoryGain** = revisit-pair consistency − gap-matched non-revisit-pair consistency (same rollout), normalized to **NMR** by the dynamic range to a short-range pair. Overall NMR correlates with human consistency judgments at Spearman ρ = 0.547 (95% CI 0.45–0.63); within-model correlation with generated motion is 0.072 vs. 0.207 for raw revisit similarity (i.e., raw similarity rewards slow/static rollouts). 7 action-conditioned models evaluated; DreamX-World-Memo highest. Code: github.com/AMAP-ML/R2MBench. — [R2M-Bench (arXiv 2608.27328)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27328)
- MIND (Feb 2026): open-domain closed-loop revisit benchmark for memory consistency and action control at 1080p/24 FPS. — [MIND (arXiv 2602.08025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08025)
- Other 2026 world-model benchmarks found: WBench (multi-turn navigation, event editing, perspective switching), PlayWorld (Aug 2026, compares WorldScore/WBench/WorldRoamBench), WorldExam (Aug 2026), and a community index. — [PlayWorld (arXiv 2608.13552)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13552); [WorldExam (arXiv 2608.02603)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02603); [World-Model-Benchmarks index](https://github.com/World-Model-Benchmarks/World-Model-Benchmarks)
- DreamX-World 1.0 (Jun 2026) acknowledges generated worlds "may drift drastically in object appearance or layout after extended interaction." — [DreamX-World 1.0 (arXiv 2606.16993)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.16993)

**FVD / FID limitations**
- Ge et al. (CVPR 2024): FVD (I3D/Kinetics features) is biased to per-frame content; a set with *more temporal inconsistency* obtained a *better* FVD (310.52) than one with spatial distortion; resampling motionless videos lowers FVD without improving temporal quality; swapping to VideoMAE-v2 features largely removes the gap ("content-debiased FVD"). On FaceForensics temporal inconsistency raised FVD by only 3.6%. — [On the Content Bias in FVD (arXiv 2404.12391)](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.12391v1)
- Luo et al. (ICLR 2025): three further limitations — non-Gaussianity of I3D feature space, insensitivity to temporal distortions, impractical sample sizes for reliable estimation; "FVD falls short as a standalone metric." — [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/a57483b394a3654f4317051e4ce3b2b8-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Beyond FVD (arXiv 2410.05203)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05203v1)
- STREAM (Mar 2024), 81 raters: FVD Spearman ρ = 0.7 (realism) and 0.5 (temporal naturalness). — [STREAM (arXiv 2403.09669)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09669)

**Imaging quality / VQA models and runtime**
- DOVER (official repo): 3.6 s per video on CPU; DOVER-Mobile 1.4 s on CPU, 9.86 M params, <1.9 GB GPU memory at inference. [OPEN] — [DOVER GitHub](https://github.com/vqassessment/DOVER)
- AIS 2024 UGC-VQA challenge: efficient learned VQA models process a 30-frame clip in ~100 ms (frame-sampled, so not a true 330 FPS). — [AIS 2024 VQA challenge (arXiv 2404.16205)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16205)
- Q-Eval-100K (CVPR 2025): 100k instances (60k images, 40k videos), 960k human annotations for quality + alignment; the *publicly retrained* Q-Eval-Score reaches video alignment PLCC 0.613 / SRCC 0.614 and video quality PLCC 0.700 / SRCC 0.673 (repo); paper reports instance-level video-quality SRCC 0.601 for its full-data model. [OPEN] — [Q-Eval GitHub](https://github.com/zzc-1998/q-eval); [Q-Eval-100K (arXiv 2503.02357)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02357v1)
- Hand/body anomaly metric: HumanScore (Apr 2026) detects temporal jitter, anatomically implausible poses, and motion drift with six interpretable kinematic/biomechanical metrics across 13 generators; finds "consistent gaps between perceptual plausibility and motion biomechanical fidelity." Human-correlation, speed and release not in the abstract. [RESEARCH] — [HumanScore (arXiv 2604.20157)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20157)
- Subject-consistency over dynamic subject sets: DynSC-Eval (Oct 2026) uses DINOv3 features + SAM2 tracking and reports local (Spike Rate, Mean Adjacent Drift) and global (Endpoint Drift, Mean Reference Drift) metrics with "substantially greater sensitivity than holistic frame-level evaluation like VBench." — [DynSC-Eval (arXiv 2610.01052)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01052)

### Inferences
- No benchmark metric in this set was designed as a per-clip pass/fail gate; the reported correlations are at model level (ranking generators over hundreds of prompts). Per-clip discriminative accuracy is much lower — see KQ2 where the best pointwise judge reaches only ~44% exact-score accuracy.
- Runtime-feasible (<1 s/clip on one GPU) components, inferred from the above: DOVER-Mobile / FAST-VQA-style imaging & aesthetic scores, DINO/CLIP/ArcFace embedding consistency, flow-based flicker (RAFT) on sampled frames. VLM-backed dimensions (LLaVA-Video-7B, Qwen2.5-VL) are seconds-scale (KQ2).
- The WorldRoamBench segment-drift definition (10 segments, best-minus-worst normalized) is directly reusable online as a streaming "is quality collapsing?" trigger using any cheap per-frame aesthetic/imaging score.
- R2M-Bench's insight (compare revisit similarity against a gap-matched control from the same rollout) is the right design for a revisit-consistency check in a game, because raw similarity rewards static cameras.

### Gaps
- VBench per-dimension evaluation runtime per clip is not published in the retrieved text; VBench-2.0's "5 min/sample" refers to video generation.
- Exact T2V-CompBench and EvalCrafter correlation tables and VBench-Long's human-correlation values were not retrieved.
- No human-correlation study specific to content-debiased FVD was found.
- WorldRoamBench leaderboard values and per-model collapse times were in the unread portion of the paper (>100k chars); not retrieved.
- MUSIQ inference speed: no source found.

## KQ2. Learned judges and VLM-as-judge: accuracy vs. humans, latency, cost, licenses

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 the strongest open video judges are VideoScore2 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Sep 2025; 44.35% pointwise accuracy / PLCC 60.37 on its own bench, beating Gemini-2.5-Pro 27.90% / GPT-5 26.24% / Claude-Sonnet-4 28.93%), Kling's VideoReward/VideoAlign (Qwen2-VL-2B, Jan 2025; best pairwise accuracy 59.69% with ties on VideoGen-RewardBench) and UnifiedReward-2.0 (Qwen3-VL 2B/8B, 2026). All run at 2 fps-sampled frames through a 2–8B VLM, i.e., roughly 1–4 s per clip on an H100-class GPU (inferred from Qwen3-VL prefill benchmarks); none publishes per-clip latency. Frontier API VLMs are *worse* than specialized 7B judges on fine-grained scoring, but cheap (~$0.004 per 10 s clip input on Gemini Flash-class pricing).

- Streaming video LLM inference (watch-and-think while frames arrive) exists as research: "Video Streaming Thinking" (Mar 2026), QuickVideo (May 2025, real-time long-video understanding via system/algorithm co-design). — [Video Streaming Thinking (arXiv 2603.12262)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12262); [QuickVideo (arXiv 2505.16175)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16175)

### Inferences
- A 2–8B VLM judge at 2 fps on a 5–10 s clip ingests ~10–20 frames (≈1–2k visual tokens at Qwen-style compression); extrapolating from the VideoChat3 numbers (1.36 s for 25k tokens), prefill should be well under 0.5 s on an H100/H200, so **total judge latency is dominated by decode of the score/reasoning tokens** — a direct-score head (VideoReward-style regression on 2B) will be ~0.2–0.5 s; a reasoning judge (VideoScore2 "think before you score") will be several seconds. None of the papers publish this; it must be measured.
- The ordering "specialized 7B judge >> frontier API VLM" on fine-grained scores suggests API VLMs should be used for *semantic/prompt-adherence yes-no questions*, not for quality scoring.
- Pairwise accuracies in the 55–75% range (with ties) mean a single judge is noisy for *absolute* gating; ensembles (Video-T1) and rubric/CoT judges (RewardVerse, VideoScore2) are the 2026 direction.
- Latent-space reward models (PAVRM, LatSearch's Qwen2-VL-3B latent RM) are the only path to judging *before* VAE decode, which matters most for real-time pipelines where decode + judge would otherwise double latency.

- No paper in this set reports per-clip wall-clock latency or VRAM for VideoScore2, VideoReward, VisionReward or UnifiedReward-2.0.

- RewardVerse / Think-then-Score / FIRM-Video result tables, sizes and weight availability were not retrievable (search budget exhausted).

## KQ3. Scene / character consistency between clip N and clip N+1, and prompt-adherence checks

### Takeaway
There is no off-the-shelf "continuity verifier"; the practical recipe assembled from 2025–26 evaluation papers is DINOv3 (or DINOv1 as in VBench) patch/global features for subject identity drift, SAM 2/3 for masks + tracking of named objects, ArcFace for character faces, SIFT/RAFT or CoTracker for multi-view/camera consistency, and VGGT (≈3–11 FPS, frame-count dependent) for geometry; R2M-Bench shows raw similarity must be calibrated against a same-rollout control or it rewards static scenes. DINOv3 and SAM 3 ship under custom Meta licenses that permit commercial use with pass-through and indemnity clauses.

### Cited Findings
- VBench Subject Consistency = DINOv1 feature similarity across frames; gameable by static output (see KQ1). — [DynSC-Eval](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01052)
- DynSC-Eval (Oct 2026): detect subjects, SAM2 segment+track visible regions, DINOv3 features per observation; local metrics Spike Rate and Mean Adjacent Drift; global metrics Endpoint Drift and Mean Reference Drift; more sensitive than VBench holistic frame-level evaluation. — [DynSC-Eval (arXiv 2610.01052)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01052)
- DINOv3 (Aug 2025): supports non-parametric label propagation for mask tracking from first-frame masks via patch-feature similarity; VGGT with a DINOv3 backbone gives "clear and consistent gains on camera pose estimation, multi-view reconstruction, and view matching." — [DINOv3 (arXiv 2508.10104)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10104v1)
- DINOv3 license: custom "DINOv3 License" (not MIT/Apache), non-exclusive worldwide royalty-free; redistribution must include the license; a GitHub issue notes the custom terms "require an ad-hoc review by legal"; Roboflow says commercial use is permitted. [OPEN, custom license] — [Meta DINOv3 License](https://ai.meta.com/resources/models-and-libraries/dinov3-license); [dinov3 issue #31](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3/issues/31); [Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/blog/train-dinov3)
- SAM 3 license ("SAM License", dated 2025-11-19): royalty-free, redistribution allowed incl. commercial with pass-through of terms; prohibits ITAR/military end uses and reverse engineering; license terminates if you sue Meta over the materials; indemnification of Meta required; California law. [OPEN, custom license] — [SAM 3 LICENSE on HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/sam3/blob/main/LICENSE); [sam3 issue #262](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam3/issues/262); [ScanCode entry](https://scancode-licensedb.aboutcode.org/sam-2025-11-19.html)
- VGGT throughput (third-party measurements, settings differ): 10.96 FPS with 16 images at 518×518 on an NVIDIA H20 96 GB; 0.36 s for 5 frames, 0.65 s for 10, 1.38 s for 20; OOM around 300 frames; a VGGT-SLAM2 variant ran 3.3 FPS on an 8 GB RTX 4060 Laptop. - Geometry-aware identity metrics exist: ConsID-Gen (Feb 2026) uses MEt3R, Chamfer Distance and DINO features on segmented objects for identity fidelity. — [ConsID-Gen (arXiv 2602.10113)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10113v1)
- VBench-2.0 implements Multi-View Consistency with SIFT + FLANN + RANSAC + RAFT, Human Identity with ArcFace/RetinaFace, Instance Preservation with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Camera Motion with CoTracker-v2 — all reusable as clip-vs-reference checks. — [VBench-2.0 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21755)
- Video Consistency Distance (Oct 2025): a frequency-domain temporal-consistency reward for I2V fine-tuning — a candidate "same scene as reference frame" scorer. — [VCD (arXiv 2510.19193)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19193)
- Revisit calibration: R2M-Bench's MemoryGain/NMR (revisit similarity minus gap-matched same-rollout control) correlates with humans at ρ = 0.547 and is far less motion-confounded than raw similarity (0.072 vs 0.207). — [R2M-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27328)
- WorldRoamBench scene memory = F1 over retained vs. hallucinated revisit observation points; subject memory via Qwen3-VL-PLUS. — [WorldRoamBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.31672v1)
- Prompt adherence in benchmarks is done with VLM VQA: VBench-2.0 uses LLaVA-Video-7B multi-question answering; T2V-CompBench uses MLLM + detection/tracking metrics (CLIP/ViCLIP/BLIP alternatives have low human correlation); Early-Failure-Detection uses ViCLIP Overall Consistency as the alignment trigger (see KQ4). — [VBench-2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21755); [T2V-CompBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14505); [Early Failure Detection (arXiv 2603.14320)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14320)
- VBench-2.0 uses YOLO-World (open-vocabulary detector) for human-part detection — precedent for grounding-model presence checks. — [VBench-2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.21755)

### Inferences
- A continuity gate for a game can be built as: (a) ArcFace cosine on tracked faces vs. character reference; (b) DINOv3 global/patch similarity of SAM-tracked key objects vs. previous-clip masks, reported as *gain over a same-scene control* (R2M-Bench logic) rather than raw thresholds; (c) VGGT or SIFT/RANSAC inlier ratio between last frame of clip N and first frame of clip N+1 for geometry; (d) a 2–3B VLM yes/no VQA ("is a wolf present?", "is the character digging?") for prompt adherence. Items (a)–(c) are sub-second on one GPU at keyframe granularity based on the cited throughputs; (d) is ~0.5–2 s.
- No paper publishes validated **thresholds** (e.g., DINO cosine cut-offs) for "same scene / same character"; these must be calibrated per game with human labels.

### Gaps
- No paper found that uses SAM 3 specifically on generated-video evaluation; DynSC-Eval uses SAM2.
- No retrieved source on Grounding DINO / OWLv2 / Florence-2 / Molmo used as prompt-adherence verifiers for T2V (search budget exhausted before this query ran).
- SigLIP2 and face-embedding accuracy for *generated* characters: no source found.
- No published CLIP/DINO similarity thresholds with human-validated precision/recall for scene continuity.

## KQ4. Latency/cost of running checks in a real-time loop; best-of-N, early-abort, proxy judging, streaming judges, fallback

### Takeaway
Best-of-N with a learned verifier gives +2–5 VBench points at N≈4–8 (Video-T1, LatSearch) with diminishing returns beyond N≈6–8, but full-video BoN multiplies generation cost by N. Two 2026 papers make the gate cheap: Early Failure Detection predicts text-alignment failure at denoising step ~10/50 with a 39.2 ms per-preview check (16–20% overhead vs. 44–51% for regeneration), and LatSearch scores partially denoised latents (steps 10–20) with a 3B latent reward model, cutting search runtime up to 79% vs. EvoSearch while beating best-of-4 VideoReward by ~1 VBench-2.0 point. No published system yet combines these with a streaming world model's rollback/"reset to last good frame"; that remains to be built.

### Cited Findings

**Best-of-N / test-time scaling gains**
- Video-T1 (Mar 2025): random linear search varied noise candidates from 1 to 30; Tree-of-Frames (ToF) 1 to 7; verifiers = VisionReward (29 weighted questions), VideoScore, VideoLLaMA3, used as a weighted ensemble; VBench used as upper-bound oracle. Best achieved VBench Total (base → +TTS): CogVideoX-5B 81.61→84.42; CogVideoX-2B 80.91→83.89; OpenSora-v1.2 79.76→81.65; Pyramid-Flow(SD3) 81.72→85.31; Pyramid-Flow(FLUX) 81.61→86.51; NOVA 78.56→79.80 (semantic score gains larger, e.g., Pyramid-Flow FLUX 71.61→82.56). Compute (GFLOPs): linear search 5.22×10⁷ vs ToF 1.62×10⁷ (Pyramid-Flow FLUX), 3.66×10⁷ vs 1.13×10⁷ (SD3), 4.02×10⁶ vs 1.41×10⁶ (NOVA) — ToF ≈3.1–3.2× cheaper than linear BoN for the same endpoint. Gains "approach a convergence limit." No wall-clock or human-eval numbers. [OPEN code] — [Video-T1 HTML (arXiv 2503.18942)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.18942v2)
- VideoScore2 best-of-5 beats random selection across six T2V models (numbers in appendix, not retrieved). — [VideoScore2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22799)
- LatSearch (Mar 2026): latent reward model on Qwen2-VL-3B with 3D-conv patchifier + step embedding, three query tokens [VQ][MQ][TA], trained to predict final VideoReward-style scores scaled by cosine(latent, final clean latent). Pairwise accuracy of the latent RM rises from ≈75.0 (step 10) to ≈77.1 (step 30). Main setting N = 6, scoring at timesteps 10/15/20; VBench-2.0 on Wan2.1-1.3B: base 51.90 → LatSearch 53.84 (+1.94), with DPM-Solver++ 55.25 (+3.35), vs. best-of-4 VideoReward 52.80 (+0.90), EvoSearch 55.01 (+3.11). N ablation: 52.81 (N=4), 53.84 (N=6), 54.13 (N=8). Runtime 164.41 s vs EvoSearch 783.76 s (−79%); vs VideoReward BoN 159 s vs 277 s (−35%). Also Wan2.1-14B 52.58→53.61, CogVideoX-2B 45.13→48.14, CogVideoX-5B 48.13→50.21. — [LatSearch (arXiv 2603.14526)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14526); [project page](https://zengqunzhao.github.io/LatSearch)
- Diagnosis-Guided Candidate Recycling (Aug 2026) critiques "generation-and-discard" TTS where unselected candidates are thrown away after costly denoising and evaluation; it recycles them. — [arXiv 2608.29322](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29322v1)
- Progressive Seed Pruning (Jul 2026, image diffusion): many seeds, prune by reward on the denoised estimate each step; reviewer notes the whole gain rests on half-denoised reward ranking ≈ final ranking. — [arXiv 2607.21591](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.21591); [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2607.21591)
- VQQA (Mar 2026): agentic video evaluation + quality improvement loop. — [VQQA (arXiv 2603.12310)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12310)

**Early-abort during generation**
- Early Failure Detection and Intervention (Mar 2026): monitors intermediate previews for text-video alignment (ViCLIP Overall Consistency); failures become predictable at **denoising step ≈11/50 (CogVideoX-5B) and ≈10 (Wan2.1-1.3B)**; Real-time Inspection costs **39.2 ms per preview** (latent-to-RGB 19.7 ms + scorer 19.5 ms) for 480p/49 frames; on 1,800 prompts flagged 872 (CogVideoX) / 811 (Wan) failures; PR-AUC for predicting other VBench metrics from Overall Consistency all > 0.5 baseline; hierarchical early-exit interventions: Trial 1 = single-frame semantic injection, Trial 2 = GPT-5-mini prompt refinement then retry. Overhead 16.6% (CogVideoX) / 20.3% (Wan) vs. 43.7% / 51.3% for regenerating failures (≈2.5–2.6× less); worst case (all three trials) ≈56% of a full regeneration. Final VBench ≈0.800 vs regeneration 0.793 (CogVideoX), 0.805 vs 0.815 (Wan). Best-of-3 comparison in appendix (not retrieved). — [Early Failure Detection (arXiv 2603.14320)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14320)

**Judging latents / low-res proxies / keyframes**
- Latent reward models that skip VAE decode: PAVRM (Nov 2025; ~84–85.5% pairwise vs VideoAlign 78.83%) and LatSearch's Qwen2-VL-3B latent RM (above). — [Latent RM (arXiv 2511.21541)](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.21541v3); [LatSearch](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14526)
- Specialized judges already sample at 2 fps (VideoScore2) and VBench-2.0 dimensions run on sampled frames; AIS-2024 VQA models reach ~100 ms per 30-frame clip by frame sampling. — [VideoScore2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.22799); [AIS 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.16205)

**Streaming world models and rewards**
- Reward Forcing (Dec 2025): streaming video generation distilled with a reward (VideoAlign motion quality) via rewarded distribution matching — reward used at *training* time for streaming models. — [Reward Forcing (arXiv 2512.04678)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04678v1)
- DreamX-World 1.0 (Jun 2026): chunk-by-chunk generation, each chunk from noise with a distilled few-step sampler and rolling KV cache, "avoids having to regenerate previous video content" — i.e., re-generating a bad chunk means discarding KV state for that chunk. — [DreamX-World HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.16993v1)
- Persistent Robot World Models (Mar 2026): RL to stabilize multi-step rollouts (drift mitigation at training time). — [arXiv 2603.25685](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25685)
- Astra (Dec 2025): interactive world model with autoregressive denoising. — [Astra (arXiv 2512.08931)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08931v1)
- Distilling physical priors into streaming world models (Aug 2026). — [arXiv 2608.07981](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07981)

**Moderation cost in the loop** (see KQ5 for details): Rekognition video moderation $0.10/min; Hive OCR-video $0.13/min, visual-video "special rates"; Sightengine frame analysis 0.5–2 fps on Starter/Pro, 0.1–10 fps Enterprise, $0.002/op overage. — [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-to-decide-between-amazon-rekognition-image-and-video-api-for-video-moderation/); [Hive pricing](https://thehive.ai/pricing); [Sightengine pricing](https://sightengine.com/pricing)

### Inferences
- Budget sketch for a 5 s clip on one H100-class GPU, from cited component speeds: imaging/aesthetic (DOVER-Mobile-class) ≈0.1–0.3 s; DINO/ArcFace continuity on 4–8 keyframes ≈0.1 s; NSFW ViT on 4–8 frames ≈0.05 s; 2–3B VLM yes/no prompt check ≈0.5–1 s; 7B reasoning judge 3–10 s. So a "fast gate" (<1 s) is feasible without the reasoning judge; the reasoning judge fits only as an asynchronous reranker or an offline auditor.
- The two 2026 early-abort results imply a concrete loop: run a 20–40 ms latent-preview check every few denoising steps from step ~10; abort and re-seed if the predicted alignment/quality is below threshold; this gives ~2.5× cheaper failure handling than regenerate-after-the-fact and avoids N× generation cost of BoN.
- For a *streaming* world model the analogous mechanism is chunk-level: score each chunk's latents (LatSearch/PAVRM-style) before decode; if it fails, drop the chunk's KV-cache entries and resample from the last accepted chunk (the "reset to last good frame" policy). WorldRoamBench's segment-drift metric and R2M-Bench's revisit-gain are the natural monitors for *when* to trigger such a reset. No paper demonstrates this end-to-end yet.
- Fallback policies (reuse cached clip, cut/transition, narrate) are not studied in the retrieved literature; they are a product-design question.

### Gaps
- No paper found that combines early-exit latent scoring with abort-and-regenerate *inside an autoregressive streaming world model*; the reliability of intermediate rewards under few-step distilled samplers is untested.
- Video-T1 per-N curves (Figs. 4–5) and human-preference results not retrievable from text; wall-clock not reported.
- Early Failure Detection's AUROC for the failure detector itself is not reported; its best-of-3 comparison is in an unread appendix.
- No source quantifies how much best-of-2 vs best-of-4 helps *human-rated* acceptability (all gains are VBench-measured).
- No published numbers on judging a low-resolution proxy vs. full-res for ranking fidelity.

### Inferences
- The only vendor-disclosed *architecture* for an output gate is OpenAI's: cheap frame classifiers at 2 fps + a custom multimodal reasoning monitor, i.e., exactly the two-tier (fast gate + slow judge) design the research literature converges on.
- Kling is the only vendor whose reward model and inference-time guidance are open; it is the de-facto default reward for 2025–26 academic RL and for best-of-N (LatSearch baseline), so it is the most defensible choice for a first reranker.
- Hosted moderation at $0.10–0.13/min is negligible relative to generation cost, but round-trip latency (unpublished) makes a local Apache-2.0 ViT classifier on sampled frames the better fit for a <1 s gate, with the hosted API as asynchronous audit.

### Gaps
- No public information on inference-time best-of-N/reranking at Runway, Veo, Sora, Kling, Pika, Luma, Decart or Odyssey.
- No latency figures published by Hive, Sightengine or Rekognition for video moderation.
- Hive's general video visual-moderation per-minute price is not public (sales only).
- LAION safety-checker latency/GPU: no source found.
- No independent evaluation of Veo 3 safety filters or SynthID robustness was retrieved (bypass claim is secondary and unverified).
